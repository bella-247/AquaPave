AquaPave Treatment Architecture

Commercial vehicle wash bays across Addis Ababa consume 100–200 liters of potable water per vehicle, discharging soapy effluent containing motor oil, road grit, and heavy metals directly onto bare soil or into city drains. This bench-scale prototype (500 × 300 mm) combines civil pervious concrete, chemical adsorption, and electromechanical separation to clean wash water entirely by gravity for on-site vehicle rewashing.

Test Footprint 500 × 300 mm
Flow Mode Passive Gravity (0 W Pumping)
Target Standard EEPA & WHO Non-Potable (<20 NTU)

Layer 01 · Structural Surface · Civil & Architectural

Pervious Concrete Slab

Cast in the AASTU civil engineering materials lab using single-sized 4.75–9.5 mm crushed basalt aggregate and Portland cement paste (water-to-cement ratio 0.36). Fine sand is intentionally omitted to form a matrix of open, interconnected pores. Cast with a 2.5% transverse slope, the slab allows dirty vehicle wash water to infiltrate immediately while withstanding vehicular wheel loads without surface puddling.

1
Interconnected Macropores (15%–35% Voids) Allows surface water to infiltrate at over 0.34 cm/s (ASTM C1701), eliminating standing wash puddles.
2
Alkaline Cement Buffering Hydrated cement phases buffer mildly acidic wash detergent (pH 8.0–9.5) and assist heavy metal precipitation.
3
2.5% Transverse Slope Prevents edge bypass and directs excess wash water evenly across the entire infiltration footprint.
Layer Thickness 80 mm Depth
Compressive Strength 14.8 MPa (ASTM C39)
Target Void Ratio 20.4% Bulk Volume
Layer 02 · Bedding & Biofilm Interface · Civil & Chemical

Choker Stone & Geotextile Membrane

Directly beneath the rigid concrete slab sits a 50 mm bedding layer of 5–10 mm choker stone, resting on a 200 g/m² non-woven polypropylene geotextile fabric. This interface supports the upper slab, creates a uniform hydraulic head, and prevents fine sand from below from migrating upward into the concrete pores. The synthetic fibers also host aerobic biofilms that begin breaking down organic wash residues.

1
5–10 mm Bedding Aggregate Distributes structural tire loads evenly while establishing a steady vertical hydraulic gradient.
2
Non-Woven Geotextile Fabric Synthetic needle-punched barrier prevents sand migration while maintaining high water permittivity (>0.5 s−¹).
3
Aerobic Biofilm Colonization High fiber surface area hosts aerobic microbes that initiate biological degradation of car-wash soap residues.
Bed Thickness 50 mm Bedding
Fabric Weight 200 g/m² Non-Woven PP
Puncture Strength >1.8 kN (ASTM D4833)
Layer 03 · Particulate Straining · Chemical & Environmental

Washed River Sand Bed

Sourced from local Ethiopian quarries, clean washed silica river sand (0.5–2.0 mm grain size, silt content <1%) serves as the primary barrier against suspended solids. Raw car-wash water contains road mud and brake grit (186–561 mg/L TSS). This 60 mm bed removes over 65% of raw particulate matter through interstitial straining, protecting the expensive activated charcoal bed below from premature blinding.

1
Washed Silica Sand Matrix Clean quartz grains with minimal fines ensure steady, non-cementing percolation under continuous wash cycles.
2
Anti-Blinding Media Barrier Arrests heavy suspended sediment (186–561 mg/L TSS) before it reaches the carbon adsorption layer.
3
Flow Equalization Diffuses concentrated wash streams uniformly across the entire cross-section of the lower treatment beds.
Layer Depth 60 mm Layer
Grain Size 0.5–2.0 mm Sieve
TSS Retention >65% Single Pass
Layer 04 · Hydrocarbon Adsorption · Chemical Engineering

Crushed Wood Charcoal Bed

Car wash wastewater carries dissolved motor oil (12–325 mg/L) and anionic surfactants from commercial soaps (~13 mg/L) that slip through simple gravel drains. Layer 4 uses locally sourced wood charcoal crushed and sieved to 1.5–4.0 mm. Its extensive internal microporous structure (400–800 m²/g) traps hydrophobic oil droplets and detergent molecules through surface adsorption.

1
Microporous Carbon Lattice Provides high-capacity surface adsorption sites that bind dissolved engine oil hydrocarbons.
2
Surfactant & Soap Attenuation Attenuates foaming detergents to destabilize and break emulsified oil-water mixtures.
3
Dual-Stage Polishing Works together with the lower pumice layer to strip residual petroleum sheen from percolating effluent.
Layer Depth 60 mm Bed
Specific Surface Area 400–800 m²/g
Oil Adsorption ~14.2 mg/g Media
Layer 05 · Mineral Sorption & Sub-Base · Chemical & Environmental

Ethiopian Rift Valley Pumice

A key local material innovation in the AquaPave design is the use of natural volcanic pumice from the Ethiopian Great Rift Valley. This vesicular rock is abundant, lightweight, and inexpensive. Its honeycomb porosity (60%–75%) serves as an internal storage reservoir during peak wash volume, while its natural alumino-silicate structure provides cation exchange capacity (25–40 meq/100g) to capture heavy metals like lead (Pb) and zinc (Zn) from brake dust.

1
Vesicular Pumice Matrix Honeycomb cavities act as a temporary surge buffer during rapid vehicle wash downpours.
2
Heavy Metal Cation Exchange Binds dissolved lead (0.53 mg/L) and zinc (1.98 mg/L) through natural mineral ion exchange.
3
Local Sourcing Advantage Replaces costly imported filtration media with locally available Rift Valley volcanic deposits.
Layer Depth 80 mm Sub-base
Total Porosity 60%–75% Bulk Volume
Cation Exchange (CEC) 25–40 meq/100g
Layer 06 · Gravity Collection · Electromechanical

Slotted Underdrain & Pea Gravel

At the base of the multi-layer bed, a 50 mm (2-inch) PVC pipe with precision 1.5 × 25 mm transverse slots collects filtered water from across the entire test footprint. The pipe is bedded in a 70 mm envelope of 10–20 mm washed pea gravel to prevent fine aggregate from choking the intake slots. Laid at a 1.5% slope, it conveys effluent smoothly into the gravity oil separator without requiring mechanical pumping.

1
Slotted PVC Collector Pipe Precision transverse slots collect filtered effluent across the entire length of the bed.
2
10–20 mm Pea Gravel Envelope Maintains open flow channels around pipe slots to prevent localized flow constriction.
3
Sampling Port Allows student teams to draw water samples directly from the underdrain to test each layer's performance.
Pipe Diameter 50 mm (2 in) Schedule 40
Gravel Depth 70 mm Envelope
Conveyance Slope 1.5% Longitudinal Fall
Layer 07 · Skimming & Telemetry · Electromechanical, ECE & Software

Baffled Separator, Reuse Tank & ESP32

Effluent from the underdrain enters a two-compartment acrylic unit. Chamber A is a Stokes' law oil-water separator with three alternating baffles that slow flow into a quiescent regime, allowing free oil droplets to float and be held behind a skimming weir. Clarified water passes into Chamber B (the reuse tank), where an ESP32 node logs optical turbidity and ultrasonic water levels in real time to alert operators before clogging occurs.

1
Baffled Stokes' Law Separator (API 421) Quiescent settling zone allows buoyant oil droplets down to 150 µm to separate and collect at the surface.
2
Clarified Non-Potable Reuse Tank Stores treated water for vehicle pre-rinsing and wheel washing (<20 NTU target).
3
ESP32 Smart Telemetry Node Continuously tracks turbidity and tank head, sending alerts if maintenance is needed.
Effluent Oil <5 mg/L Target
Turbidity <20 NTU Reuse
Telemetry ESP32 · Ultrasonic · Turbidity
System Integration · Full Operating Train · IETP 4115 Verification

Full Treatment Train Flow Simulation

Reassembled in its operating configuration, simulated wash influent carrying 186–561 mg/L TSS, 12–325 mg/L oil, and 187–483 NTU turbidity percolates continuously through the complete prototype. The combined sequence—surface straining, depth sand filtration, carbon adsorption, pumice ion exchange, and gravity skimming—produces clarified water suitable for reuse, saving an estimated 1,000–2,000 liters of potable municipal water per commercial bay daily.

Turbidity Reduction 187–483 → <20 NTU
Water Savings 1,000–2,000 L/day per bay
UN SDG Alignment SDG 6 (Clean Water), 9 & 11
