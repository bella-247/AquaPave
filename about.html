Section 2 · Engineering case

About the Project

System architecture, treatment mechanisms, governing equations, main components, and expected outcomes.

01 · Context and motivation

Project overview and problem statement

Between 2005 and 2025, built-up urban land in Addis Ababa expanded from 42.5% to 62.6%, nearly doubling surface runoff from 0.54 million m³/day to 1.03 million m³/day. At the same time, the Addis Ababa Water and Sewerage Authority meets only about 40% of the city's 1.2 million m³/day potable water demand.

Commercial garages and car-wash stations consume 100–200 L of tap water per vehicle and discharge untreated wastewater carrying motor oil (12–325 mg/L), suspended solids (186–561 mg/L TSS), turbidity (187–483 NTU), anionic surfactants (median 13 mg/L), and heavy metals (Pb = 0.53 mg/L, Zn = 1.98 mg/L) directly into roadside drains.

Because commercial soaps emulsify oil droplets, standard gravity skimming traps achieve under 30%–40% removal efficiency. Just one liter of waste oil can contaminate up to 1,000,000 liters of water, while oily sludge blocks storm channels and triggers localized urban flooding.

Water scarcity Untreated discharge Drain blockage Non-potable reuse

Central research question

How can a low-cost permeable wash-pad pavement treat garage wastewater in Addis Ababa so that it can be reused for non-potable purposes, and how can its hydraulic and water-quality performance be reliably monitored?

Scope boundaries

In scope: a bench-scale permeable wash-pad prototype, multi-layer filter bed, baffled oil separator, reuse storage tank, ESP32 water-level and turbidity monitoring, and triplicate testing against a dense concrete control slab.

Out of scope: full-scale commercial construction, potable drinking-water treatment (the system is strictly for non-potable reuse), and multi-year field durability testing.

SDG compliance alignment

Primary goals: SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation — Targets 6.3 and 6.4), SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure — Target 9.1), and SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities — Target 11.5).

Supporting goals: SDG 1 (No Poverty — low-cost local materials and water savings) and SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy — passive gravity treatment with no treatment pumps and a low-power sensor node).

03 · System architecture

Proposed system and operational flow

AquaPave replaces a conventional impermeable wash apron with a sequential, gravity-driven treatment train. All edges are sealed with a flexible waterproof liner to prevent wall bypass.

→
Stage 2 Choker + geotextile Fine-solids interception and biofilm support
→
Stage 3 Adsorbent media Sand, charcoal, and volcanic pumice

Stage 1 · Surface

Sloped apron and pervious concrete slab

Wash water discharged across a 2%–5% sloped apron infiltrates through a no-fines pervious concrete slab designed to ACI 522R-10 (water-to-cement ratio 0.27–0.34, target void content 15%–35%). The surface strains coarse road grit, while alkaline cement hydration buffers pH to 8.0–9.5 and helps precipitate dissolved heavy metals as insoluble hydroxides and carbonates. Perimeter edges are sealed with a flexible waterproof liner.

ACI 522R-10 · ASTM C1701
Stage 2 · Interface

Choker course and non-woven geotextile

A 5–10 mm aggregate choker course stabilizes the slab above a non-woven geotextile membrane. The geotextile traps fine suspended silt and supports hydrocarbon-degrading microbial biofilms, contributing to mechanical filtration and in-situ biological treatment.

TSS interception · biofilm support
Stage 3 · Media bed

Multi-layer sub-base adsorbent bed

Water percolates downward through stratified beds of washed sand (mechanical sieving of fine TSS), crushed wood charcoal or activated carbon (adsorption of emulsified oils and anionic surfactants), and local volcanic pumice (cation exchange for dissolved lead and zinc).

Adsorption · cation exchange
Stage 4 · Collection

Perforated PVC underdrain

A slotted PVC underdrain collects filtered water from the base of the waterproofed containment tray and routes it under laminar flow toward the separation chamber. Pipe slots and end caps are sized to pass the design flow without creating a hydraulic bottleneck.

Laminar collection
Stage 5 · Separation

Baffled gravity oil separation chamber

The chamber is sized using Stokes' law so that residual free oil droplets can rise to the surface for skimming. Baffles slow the flow and reduce short-circuiting. The idealized design target is a minimum surface area of 0.04 m² for 100 µm droplets at a flow of 2 L/min.

Stokes' law · API 421
Stage 6 · Reuse and monitoring

Non-potable reuse tank and ESP32 node

Clarified effluent overflows into a reuse container for non-potable vehicle pre-rinsing or floor washing. An ESP32 microcontroller paired with water-level and turbidity sensors logs effluent clarity, tank volume, and drain time, and triggers a maintenance alert when surface pores clog.

Clarity · level · clogging ratio
04 · Components and equations

Main system components and governing equations

The prototype combines structural pavement, chemical filtration, environmental monitoring, and low-power electronics. Performance is evaluated with standard test methods and a transparent water balance.

Main system components and their engineering functions.
Subsystem or layer Material and technical specification Primary engineering function
Permeable wash surface No-fines Portland cement pervious concrete: water-to-cement ratio 0.27–0.34, 15%–35% voids, single-size coarse aggregate; modular permeable pavers as backup. Supports structural load, eliminates surface runoff, strains coarse grit, and buffers pH to 8.0–9.5.
Bedding and geotextile 5–10 mm choker aggregate, 2–5 mm fine stone, and non-woven geotextile fabric. Mechanical sieving of fine TSS, free oil-slick interception, and biofilm support.
Adsorbent filter media Stratified washed sand, crushed wood charcoal or activated carbon, and volcanic pumice. Adsorbs emulsified oils and surfactants; immobilizes dissolved lead and zinc through cation exchange.
Containment and plumbing Sloped washing tray (2%–5%), flexible waterproof perimeter liner, and slotted PVC underdrain pipe with end caps. Prevents edge bypass and collects percolated sub-base effluent.
Oil separator and reuse tank Baffled gravity separation box sized for at least 0.04 m² at 100 µm droplets and 2 L/min; non-potable storage tank. Separates residual free-phase hydrocarbons and stores clarified water for vehicle pre-rinsing.
Smart monitoring node ESP32 microcontroller, water-level sensor, turbidity sensor, signal-conditioning circuit, and low-power or solar supply. Continuous data acquisition, turbidity and level logging, and hydraulic clogging alert generation.

05 · Benchmarks and outcomes

Expected outcomes and non-potable reuse targets

The numerical targets below are design benchmarks to be tested during the experimental phase. They are not pre-claimed results, and the comparison with reuse limits is indicative rather than a regulatory certification.

Expected engineering outcomes

Hydraulic performance

Complete elimination of edge bypass during a one-hour leak check, slab infiltration rates exceeding the dosing rate by at least five times, and substantially lower runoff than the dense concrete control slab.

Pollutant removal

At least 80% turbidity reduction, consistent with dual-stage sand/gravel and activated carbon benchmarks of roughly 90% TSS and 86% oil and grease removal, and no visible oil film in the final reuse tank.

Potable water conservation

On-site recovery capable of saving 1,000–2,000 L/day of municipal tap water for a small garage washing 20 vehicles per day at 50% recycling.

Clogging rejuvenation

Quantitative demonstration of surface permeability recovery after brushing or pressure-washing maintenance, tracked with the clogging ratio Rc.
